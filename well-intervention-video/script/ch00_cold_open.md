## 0.01 | 43.5 | anim
VO: This well has been producing oil for eleven years. Two years ago it made a thousand cubic metres a day. Today it makes six hundred. The reservoir is healthy, the pressure is fine, and the fault is somewhere inside the well itself: a steel pipe fourteen centimetres wide and four kilometres long, that nobody can look into. [pause 0.5] So what do you do? You cannot send a person down. You cannot open it, because it is full of oil at two hundred bar. All you can do is lower something into it, on a wire or a pipe, and work blind.

## 0.02 | 37 | anim
VO: This is well intervention: going back into a well that is already built, and usually still producing, to look inside it, clean it, repair it, change it, or close it. It is how the industry keeps wells alive for decades. And it comes down to two questions. Why do we go back into a well? And how: on a wire, on a coiled steel tube, or with a rig? This film answers both, in that order.
