# The template interface: when has it failed? — transcript

NORSOK U-001:2026, Annex B. Running time 11:18. Timestamps are mm:ss.


## 00:00  Cold open

**[00:00]** This is where the well meets the template. The conductor housing lands in a slot of the template structure, and lower down, the conductor is guided by a ring in the bottom of the structure. When the riser pulls on the wellhead, the conductor pushes on that ring.  
**[00:21]** So when has this interface failed, according to the standard? When the steel reaches 0.2 % plastic strain, the yield strength? Or at a strain limit, like the 5 %, or the 2 %, that people attribute to Eurocode 3, part 1-5?  
**[00:40]** In this follow-up to the acceptance criteria video, we find out which standard owns the interface, what failure means on each side of it, what Eurocode 3 actually says, and we check the slot ring both ways.  

## 00:55  01 Who owns the interface

**[00:58]** NORSOK U-001 names the contacts in Figure J.1. Contact C is between the template structure and the conductor housing, and contact D is at the bottom of the structure. When the template is in the load path, load path three runs from the wellhead, through the conductor, into the guide structure and down to the seabed. How much load goes that way is the load share. It depends on the tolerances and on the relative stiffness of well, template and soil.  
**[01:33]** The interface has two sides, and each side has its own rule book. The wellhead side, the conductor housing and the conductor, is checked to Annex B of U-001 and the pipe formulas of clause B.9. That was the first video: the yield strength at 0.2 % plastic strain, and capacities found as loads. The template side is different. Clause 13.3.6.1 says that structural design shall follow ISO 13628-15, clause 5.4, and NORSOK N-001. N-001 sends steel design on to N-004, and N-004 builds on Eurocode 3. So U-001 has no failure criterion of its own for the template steel. It hands it over.  
**[02:33]** Before any stress, the interface can fail as a mechanism. The well grows with temperature, and the template settles. Clause 13.3.6.2 asks the template design to account for a relative vertical displacement of 100 millimetres between template and conductor, unless otherwise specified. Note d of Table A.2 makes this a serviceability check, with load factor 1.0: the margins to the upper and lower end stops are checked. If an end stop is reached, the template is preloaded, and that force goes into separate ultimate limit state load cases. And the installed template may not lean more than 0.3 degrees, unless the tolerance loops allow it.  

## 03:24  02 The template steel: the elastic check

**[03:28]** On the template side there are no design factors. It is load and resistance factor design. The loads are multiplied by the load factors of N-001, which Table A.2 lists: 1.3 on the extreme riser loads in the ultimate limit state, 1.0 in the accidental case. The resistance is divided by a material factor: 1.15 for steel in the ultimate limit state, in N-004, and 1.0 in the accidental limit state.  
**[04:04]** And the yield strength? For a 40 millimetre plate of S355, the nominal yield strength, fy, is 345 MPa. Eurocode 3 takes it from the product standard: the upper yield strength, or the 0.2 % proof strength when the steel shows no distinct yield point. So yes, it is the same idea as the 0.2 % offset. And it is reached at a strain of only 0.16 %.  
**[04:39]** The first Eurocode check is elastic. Clause 6.2.1, paragraph five, of part 1-1: the von Mises stress at the critical point shall not exceed fy divided by γM0. Here is the lower slot ring, cut from that plate, with the conductor pushing on it between two struts. At the design contact force of 1.6 MN, the outer edge behind the contact reaches 416 MPa. The limit is 300. Utilisation, 1.39. So by the elastic check, the ring has failed. But that check stops at the first point that yields. It is the most conservative option, not the only one.  

## 05:30  03 Non-linear FE: EN 1993-1-5 Annex C

**[05:34]** For a plated detail in a finite element model, Eurocode 3 offers another route: Annex C of part 1-5, finite element methods of analysis. It is an informative annex, so the National Annex and your design basis decide whether it is used. For a stocky part that cannot buckle, like this ring, a materially non-linear analysis is enough. Where buckling is possible, the geometric non-linearity and the imperfections must be included as well.  
**[06:07]** Clause C.6 gives the material models: elastic plastic without strain hardening, with a nominal plateau slope, with linear strain hardening, or a true stress strain curve from tests. They use characteristic values: fy itself, not divided by the material factor. Clause C.7 puts the factored design loads into the model, multiplied by one load multiplier, α, which the analysis increases until a limit state is reached.  
**[06:39]** And here is the clause you heard about: C.8, the limit state criteria. Where the structure is susceptible to buckling, the criterion is reaching the maximum load. In regions in tension, it is reaching a limiting value of the principal membrane strain. Note one: the National Annex may specify that value, and 5 % is recommended. Note two: other criteria may be used, for example reaching the yield criterion, or limiting the yielded zone. Membrane means the strain at mid thickness of the shell, not the surface value with plate bending added on top.  
**[07:20]** Let's run it. Up to 83 % of the design load, the ring is elastic. Then the outer edge behind the contact yields, and at 1.25 times the design load it has 0.2 % plastic strain. The plastic zones spread, behind the contact and at the struts. Without strain hardening, the curve flattens at 1.54: a plastic mechanism. With linear hardening, it keeps rising. There is no peak and no buckling, so the 5 % strain decides: at 1.76 times the design load.  
**[08:01]** Clause C.9 sets the margin. The multiplier at the limit state, αu, shall be at least α1 times α2. α1 covers the uncertainty of the FE model, calibrated against tests. α2 covers the scatter of loads and resistance: γM1 where instability governs, γM2 where fracture governs. With α1 equal to one, for a benchmarked model, and α2 no more than 1.3, αu of 1.76 passes, and so does 1.53 without hardening. So the ring that failed the elastic check is accepted by the non-linear one, with margin.  

## 08:49  04 Five percent, and the two

**[08:52]** Now the 2 %. Annex C contains one strain value, the recommended 5 %. We found no 2 % criterion in part 1-5. If someone quotes one, ask which document it comes from, and which strain it limits. And what would it change here? With hardening, stopping at 2 % instead of five lowers the capacity by 10 %. Without hardening, by only 2 %, because the curve is already flat.  
**[09:24]** There is a catch with every strain limit. Without hardening, the same load, 1.5 times the design load, gives 1.4 % strain with coarse elements, and 2.4 % with fine ones. The load at the limit hardly moves, but the strain does. With hardening, the mesh no longer matters. So a strain limit always comes with a material model, a mesh, and a definition of the strain. The second generation of Eurocode 3 develops this into a part of its own, EN 1993-1-14, published in 2025. Check which edition your design basis calls for.  

## 10:09  05 Summary

**[10:13]** So, when has the template interface failed? As a mechanism, when the relative displacement uses up the margin to the end stops. On the wellhead side, when the load exceeds Fd times the capacity of Annex B, with the yield strength at 0.2 % plastic strain. On the template side, by the elastic route, when the von Mises stress at a point reaches fy over γM. And by the non-linear route, at the maximum load or at 5 % principal membrane strain, with αu at least α1 times α2. Two percent is not a criterion of Eurocode 3, part 1-5.  
**[10:57]** The ring, its load and the factors in this video are teaching inputs. The clauses are on screen, and the notes list what was checked and against which source. Use the editions and the National Annex of your own design basis.
